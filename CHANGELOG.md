# Changelog

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

Versions follow Home Assistant's scheme, `YEAR.MONTH.PATCH` (for example `2026.10.0`), with `bN` suffixes for betas. Beta versions are not published as releases.

## [Unreleased]

### Fixed

- New Zealand entries no longer poll MetService more often than its CAP Code of Practice allows: during a threat the coordinator polls every minute, but a MetService result younger than two minutes is reused (its alerts are still re-checked for expiry) (#62).
- Queensland home-zone entries no longer always poll the Moreton Bay dashboard: a new entry gets the nearest preset council dashboard within 40 km of home (a geographic heuristic, not council boundaries), or none. Existing home-zone entries that still have the automatic Moreton Bay URL are migrated the same way (config entry version 1.2); a URL set in the options is kept and can be changed or cleared there (#19).
- Home-zone entries follow the Home Assistant home location: coordinates are no longer copied into the entry, and the coordinator (and the providers) use the current home location at every refresh. Reconfiguring an entry with coordinates turns it into a fixed-location entry (#19).
- Camera entities follow the data: cameras that appear are added and cameras that disappear are removed (their registry entries are kept, so a returning camera keeps its entity ID), and cameras outside the radius or listed twice are dropped. The four hard-coded Brisbane camera entities are removed from the entity registry by the 1.2 migration (#19).
- Tasmanian alerts come from TasALERT's `data.geojson` (the feed its map uses) instead of the coordinate-less RSS feed, so every incident is placed at its own location instead of a fixed Hobart point; closed incidents and alerts without coordinates are skipped, and fuel reduction burns are flagged as hazard reduction. ACT ESA times such as `15 Oct 2026 15:14:49.77` are now parsed (#14).
- New Zealand Civil Defence (NEMA) alerts are no longer all dropped: each AlertHub Atom entry's escaped CAP alert is parsed, cancelled/expired/non-`Actual` alerts are skipped, alerts are placed inside their CAP area with distance measured to it, and Red or `Extreme` alerts are Emergency Warnings. MetService warnings are read from their CAP documents (cached): level from `ColourCode` (Red/Orange/Yellow), area from `areaDesc`/polygon instead of forecast prose, superseded and expired warnings dropped; located warnings become hazards and the rest stay weather warnings. GeoNet quake levels follow felt intensity (MMI), so deep quakes are no longer escalated by magnitude, and volcano alert levels no longer report a fake "now" timestamp (#11).
- BoM warnings are no longer placed at the configured home location. The WMO feed is treated as a list of pointers to CAP documents (fetched once and cached), and only the newest message per BoM product counts. Cancelled, expired and non-`Actual` messages are dropped, and ACT matches NSW (`IDN`) products. A warning with CAP area geometry becomes a hazard located at its area, with distance measured to the area (0 when home is inside it). BoM CAP documents currently identify areas only by AMOC area codes, so such warnings appear in the weather-warnings sensor instead of as hazards. On the test instance this removed 57 statewide or expired warnings that were reported 0 km away (#10).
- Queensland Fire Department warnings read the fields the live feed actually has: the hazard type comes from `GroupedType`/`EventType` (permitted burns are flagged as hazard reduction), `updated_at` from the item time `ItemDateTimeLocal_ISO` (Brisbane), the location from the `Latitude`/`Longitude` properties, the place from `Location`/`WarningArea`, and the status from `CurrentStatus`. Agency and link are fixed QFD values (#12).
- Council road hazards from `/dashboard/tmrRoadData` are decoded (the dashboard returns JSON inside a JSON string), so QLDTraffic events near the council appear again, with their road, locality and update time (#12).
- Council dashboards work beyond Moreton Bay. A shared Guardian IMS client (`providers/guardian_ims.py`), used by QLD and NSW, reads the endpoints every dashboard has: Ergon outages (with their swapped `Lat`/`Long` corrected) and Energex outages, `imsRoad` council roads, `imsEvac` evacuation centres, places of refuge and sandbag points (with their real status instead of a hard-coded "Open"), BoM observations for the stations the dashboard's own weather widget lists, air quality for the stations its air-quality widget lists, and `getCameras`. Moreton Bay's own endpoints are still read where they exist. An endpoint a dashboard does not have (404/403) is not requested again for six hours instead of every poll, and reopened QLDTraffic roads are not listed as closures (#25).
- The council `/dashboard/bom` summary (for example "Marine Wind Warning Summary for Queensland") is no longer turned into a hazard pinned to the configured location at 0 km; it is listed as a weather warning only. Council bulletins without geometry are likewise news only (#25).
- The fire danger rating keeps the forecast day it applies to (in the evening the feed already gives tomorrow's rating), and `total_fire_ban` is `null` when the dashboard does not publish a total fire ban field, instead of claiming "no ban" (#25).
- Dead and moved council hosts: the Gold Coast and Redland presets use `dashboard.goldcoast.qld.gov.au` and `dashboard.redland.qld.gov.au` (saved entries with the old URLs are redirected automatically), the Sunshine Coast preset no longer polls Disaster Hub (it is not a Guardian dashboard), and the council link lists drop Brisbane's and Bega Valley's unresolvable hosts and Tweed (not Guardian) and add Byron and Northern Beaches (#12, #25).
- WA fire danger rating now reads the feed's real fields (`description`, `area`, `forecasts["0"].fireDanger`) for the configured location's fire weather district, resolved with bundled Bureau of Meteorology `IDM00007` district polygons, instead of always reporting North Kimberley's (or a default "Moderate") rating. A location outside every WA district reports no rating. The feed has no total fire ban field, so `total_fire_ban` is now unknown for WA instead of a false "no ban" (#14).
- NT no longer reports closed incidents (`_status`/`Status` "closed", most of the feed on a typical day) as active hazards (#14).
- ACT no longer reports ambulance dispatches as emergency hazards. The `<type>` element now drives the hazard type and the hazard-reduction flag (#14).
- SA warning levels come from the CAP-AU `WarningLevel` parameter (Emergency Warning, Watch and Act, Advice; plain incidents are Information) instead of `cap:severity`, which the feed always sends as "Unknown"; every SA hazard was Information, so an SA Emergency Warning never turned on the emergency warning sensor. The agency is the CAP `senderName` instead of the sender email, and the status and link come from the entry (#27).
- VIC no longer emits burn-area (burn scar) polygons as hazards, drops Bureau of Meteorology items that the BoM provider already reports, and links each item to VicEmergency as `https://emergency.vic.gov.au/respond/#!/{feedType}/{id}/moreinfo` instead of a `/warning/` route for every item (#27).
- The setup form suggests the right state near borders. South Australia is limited to south of 26°S, so western Queensland (e.g. Mount Isa) is no longer suggested as SA. Victoria uses a simplified boundary polygon (Murray River and the SA/NSW borders, from the ABS state boundary), so Murray River towns such as Mildura, Wodonga, Moama and Buronga and southern NSW towns such as Albury, Wagga Wagga and Eden get the right default (#6).
- Reconfigure writes the radius, overrides and council dashboard URL to the entry options as well, because options override entry data at runtime; previously a value set in Options won over the reconfigured one. Reconfigure updates the entry's unique ID and title when the state or location changes and aborts with `already_configured` if another entry already covers that location. Out-of-range values return a translated form error instead of raising (#6).
- `geo_location` entities get a unique ID (`<entry_id>_<hazard_id>`) like the core feed integrations, keep their last known headline and coordinates while being removed (no more "Emergency Incident" placeholder), and their registry entries are removed when the incident ends, including incidents that ended while Home Assistant was stopped (#18).
- Feed outages are no longer invisible. A provider that cannot fetch or decode its feed now reports itself unavailable instead of returning "no hazards". The coordinator keeps that feed's last good hazards for up to 30 minutes (so a cancelled warning cannot linger through a long outage), logs once when a feed goes down and once when it recovers, and raises a translated `UpdateFailed` when every hazard feed is down, so entities become unavailable (and setup retries) instead of showing "All Clear". One malformed record now skips only itself, not the rest of its feed. Coordinator data exposes `feeds_ok`/`feeds_failed` (#16).
- Agency timestamps without a time zone are interpreted in the agency's local zone (Brisbane, Sydney, Melbourne, Adelaide, Perth, Hobart, Darwin, Auckland) instead of UTC, so `updated_at` is no longer 8–13 hours off. Zone abbreviations such as `AEDT` are understood, ACT uses the incident's "Updated:" time, and NSW RFS parses `UPDATED` as Sydney time. Unparsable or missing times are now omitted instead of being reported as "now" (#13).
- Hazard IDs are deterministic: NT incidents no longer use list position or a 20-character location prefix (which collided), the BoM fallback no longer uses the per-process `hash()`, WA items without an ID no longer collapse into one, and QLD relief facilities and NZ NEMA alerts without an upstream ID get stable fallbacks (OBJECTID or a content hash). This stops `geo_location` entity churn after restarts and feed reordering (#7).
- Declare the third-party packages the integration imports (`defusedxml>=0.7.1`, `pydantic>=2.10`) in `manifest.json`, so installs without them no longer fail to load (#17).
- CI, release and PR-governance workflows now target `custom_components/ausnz_disaster_watch/`; release creation and the committed-bundle check work again (#9).
- Manifest documentation, issue tracker and codeowner, README links, HACS repository URL and `hacs.json` name now match `tinkerologie/ha-ausnz-disaster-watch` (#9).
- Hassfest validation gets `contents: read`; with `permissions: {}` it could not check out the private repository and always failed. HACS validation is skipped while the repository is private, because HACS cannot read or install private repositories (#9).
- Lovelace card TypeScript build errors (undeclared sensor variables) and the weather observation entity lookup (#4).

### Changed

- QLD no longer reads the council `fireDetailed` feed: it is a statewide list without coordinates. QLD cameras now come from the council dashboard (`getCameras`) instead of four hard-coded Brisbane cameras; the old camera entities are no longer provided and can be removed (#25, #19).
- **BREAKING CHANGE:** `geo_location` events now have `source: ausnz_disaster_watch` instead of the agency name, as Home Assistant's `geo_location` trigger and map card expect one source per integration. The agency is in the new `agency` attribute; update automations or cards that filtered on an agency name in `source` (#18).
- **BREAKING CHANGE:** `geo_location` entity IDs are `geo_location.ausnz_disaster_watch_<hazard_id>` (long IDs are shortened with a digest) instead of being built from the full warning headline. The headline stays the entity name (#18).
- Northern Territory incidents come from the NT Government `Hosted/Fire_Incidents` ArcGIS FeatureServer (Bushfires NT and NTFRS point layers, active incidents only) instead of the PFES `incidents.json` file, which says "Please do not use, scrape or re-publish this file". Closed incidents are still skipped, IDs stay stable (notified time, type and location; colliding incidents are separated by position), one failing layer keeps the other, and public advice text (risks, what to do, current situation) comes from the matching burnt-area polygon. The service is refreshed from the PFES data about hourly, so NT incidents can lag the PFES map by up to an hour (#34).
- GeoNet quakes and volcanic alert levels and NSW RFS major incidents are fetched and decoded by the maintained `aio_geojson_geonetnz_quakes`, `aio_geojson_geonetnz_volcano` and `aio_geojson_nsw_rfs_incidents` libraries (the ones Home Assistant core uses) over Home Assistant's shared session, instead of hand-written GeoJSON parsing. Lower bounds match core's pins (`>=0.16`, `>=0.9`, `>=0.7`). The feed failure contract is unchanged: a failed fetch, an HTTP error or a document that is not a FeatureCollection makes the source unavailable, and one malformed record (including broken geometry or an unusual timestamp) skips only itself. Hazard IDs and fields are unchanged, and so are levels except that NSW RFS incidents with no warning level ("Not Applicable", "Planned Burn") are now Information instead of Unknown; NSW RFS descriptions are still split locally because the library misses the last field and has no ALERT LEVEL or UPDATED. GeoNet hazards now credit Earth Sciences New Zealand instead of GNS Science (#35).
- `…_fire_danger_rating` now comes from the BoM fire danger forecast for your district. Council dashboard (NSW, QLD) and Emergency WA ratings are used only when that forecast has no rating for today. The state values and existing attributes are unchanged (#28).
- Option changes are applied by reloading the entry (`OptionsFlowWithReload`) and the update listener is removed; Home Assistant 2026.12 breaks integrations that combine an update listener with `async_update_reload_and_abort` (#15).
- The coordinator is stored in typed `entry.runtime_data` instead of `hass.data[DOMAIN]`, receives its config entry explicitly, and initialises its base class before reading config values, so entries without coordinates fall back to the Home Assistant home location instead of raising `AttributeError` (#5, #8).
- Entity names come from translations (`_attr_translation_key`) instead of hard-coded `_attr_name`; names are unchanged. Imports use public Home Assistant modules (#8).
- Versioning follows Home Assistant (`YEAR.MONTH.PATCH`); the manifest is `2026.10.0b0`. The release workflow accepts that scheme and does not publish alpha, beta or release-candidate versions, so merging changes during the beta creates no release.
- CI uploads test coverage to Codecov (`codecov.yml`: project coverage may not drop, patch coverage 100%) (#9).
- New `script/check-imports` and CI job verify every module imports in a clean Home Assistant environment with only the declared requirements (#17).
- README entity tables, card type and automation examples (modern `triggers`/`actions` syntax, real entity IDs) updated; AGENTS.md/GEMINI.md/Copilot instructions describe the real package layout and domain (#9).
- Browser regression test steps removed from CI until the card suite rebuild adds Playwright tests (#43).

### Added

- Data source register (`docs/development/DATA_SOURCES.md`): every endpoint the integration calls, its owner, licence or terms (checked 2026-10-16), attribution, commercial use, rate limits and status, plus the decisions for sources with restrictive terms (VicEmergency, TasALERT, SA, BoM, council dashboards). A test fails when a URL in the providers is missing from the register (#34).
- Entities credit their data sources: sensors, binary sensors and cameras show an `attribution` for the entry's sources, and map pins show the attribution of the feed the incident came from. Diagnostics list the sources (owner, licence, terms URL, status) (#34).
- Opt-in forecast signals from Open-Meteo, all off by default and kept apart from agency warnings (plain sensors with `official: false`, never hazards or warning levels). Each has its own poll interval and one request per poll; an Open-Meteo outage only makes that feature's entities unavailable. The options flow is now a menu (monitoring settings, forecast signals, river points). Attribution, CC BY 4.0 and the non-commercial free-tier limits are documented in the README.
  - Weather hazard signals, hourly: maximum wind gust and apparent temperature for the next 24 hours with damaging-gusts (default 90 km/h) and extreme-heat (default 40 °C) binary sensors, a thunderstorm potential level from CAPE, precipitation probability and weather code (a heuristic, not a BoM category), and a calculated McArthur FFDI labelled not official, with an experimental drought factor (#39).
  - River flood forecast at up to 10 user-selected points, every 6 hours: GloFAS discharge sensor with a 7-day forecast and trend, and a flood-risk binary sensor. Points off the river grid get a nearby-cell suggestion; the default threshold is the cell's median annual peak discharge from the GloFAS reanalysis (#38).
  - Experimental AI ensemble rain outlook, every 6 hours: heavy-rain probability over 24 and 72 hours and ensemble-mean rain from ECMWF AIFS, plus Google WeatherNext 2 behind its own toggle (fetched at most every 12 hours) (#40).
- New Zealand road closures from NZ Transport Agency Waka Kotahi (`trafficnz.info` Traffic Application Server, no key). Events are requested for a box around home, so `road_closures_count` and `road_closure_nearby` work for NZ entries. Closures, road hazards (crashes, slips) and unplanned warnings (flooding, snow) count; planned roadworks with only caution or delays, and resolved or scheduled events, do not. A closure along a highway is placed at its point nearest home (#24).
- GeoNet quake CAP alerts: a quake GeoNet has issued a CAP alert for (moderate or stronger shaking, suitable for alerting) carries the alert's headline, intensity and expiry, and its distance is measured to the alert's felt-intensity area (0 inside it). The level still follows MMI (#11). A CAP feed outage never fails the quakes; the last good alerts are kept for up to 30 minutes (#24).
- README "Data Sources & Attribution" section (GeoNet CC BY 3.0 NZ, NEMA, MetService, NZTA, NSW RFS) (#24).
- Fire danger outlook and total fire bans for every Australian state and territory, and fire season for New Zealand (#28). The configured location is matched to its BoM fire weather district with bundled `IDM00007` boundaries for all 139 districts (replacing the WA-only file). The Bureau of Meteorology fire danger forecast for that state (`IDN10016`, `IDV18555`, `IDQ13016`, `IDS10070`, `IDW15100`, `IDT13151`, `IDD10731`) provides a 4-day `forecast` attribute on `…_fire_danger_rating`, plus `fire_behaviour_index`, `date`, `issued_at` and `source`; NSW and QLD no longer need a council dashboard, and ACT, VIC, SA, TAS and NT get a rating for the first time. Days are dated in the district's local time, and an afternoon forecast that starts tomorrow keeps today's rating from the morning issue, so the evening never shows tomorrow as today. The new `binary_sensor.…_total_fire_ban` (Australian entries) reads bans from NSW RFS, ACT ESA, CFA, SA CFS and Emergency WA (by local government area); where no ban feed is read (QLD, NT, TAS) it is unknown rather than off. New Zealand entries get `sensor.…_fire_season` (`open`, `restricted`, `prohibited`) with Section 52 prohibitions. Fire data is polled at most every 30 minutes, separately from hazards, and a failed poll keeps the last good outlook. The new sources are in the data source register (`bom_fire_danger`, `cfa_fire_bans`, `sa_cfs_fire_danger`, `abs_lga`, `fenz_fire_season`, plus the NSW RFS, ACT ESA and Emergency WA rows), and the fire entities credit the sources for their district.
- River and creek level sensors (opt-in). The options flow is now a menu (**Monitoring settings**, unchanged, and **River and creek level gauges**); the gauge step lists up to 10 of the nearest gauges within the radius that reported a level in the last 6 hours, from the Queensland Water Monitoring Information Portal and WaterNSW (Kisters Hydstra web services) and eight New Zealand regional council Hilltop servers. Each selected gauge becomes a water level sensor in metres with `trend`, `change_1h_m`, `observed_at` and `distance_km` attributes, polled every 15 minutes by its own coordinator. Gauges without a reading in the last 6 hours are unavailable, a gauge service outage keeps the last reading until it is 6 hours old, and unselected gauges' entities are removed. An optional alert level (m) per gauge adds a `problem` binary sensor that is on while the level is at or above it (`alert_level_m` attribute); no open source publishes flood classification levels, so there are no built-in thresholds (#31).
- **Australia and New Zealand Disaster Watch Integration**: Complete real-time hazard, disaster, and emergency incident monitoring integration for Home Assistant across Australian jurisdictions and New Zealand.
  - Nationwide Australian and New Zealand coverage:
    - **Queensland (QLD)**: QFD incident feed, BoM telemetry, and local Council Disaster Dashboards (Moreton Bay, Sunshine Coast, Brisbane, Gold Coast, Cairns, Townsville, etc.).
    - **New South Wales (NSW)**: NSW RFS bushfire and NSW SES storm/flood incident feeds.
    - **Victoria (VIC)**: EMV OSOM (VicEmergency) multi-agency emergency incidents.
    - **Western Australia (WA)**: Emergency WA / DFES official warnings, active CAD incidents, and fire danger ratings.
    - **South Australia (SA)**: SA SAFECOM / CFS CAP-AU XML warning and incident feed.
    - **Tasmania (TAS)**: TasALERT multi-agency emergency alert feed.
    - **Northern Territory (NT)**: Bushfires NT and NT Fire and Rescue Service incidents from the NT Government Fire_Incidents service.
    - **Australian Capital Territory (ACT)**: ACT Emergency Services Agency (ESA) GeoRSS feed.
    - **New Zealand (NZ)**: GeoNet earthquakes REST API, GeoNet volcanic activity alerts, NEMA AlertHub CAP Atom feed, and MetService severe weather warning RSS.
    - **Federal (BoM)**: Bureau of Meteorology national and statewide warning summaries.
  - Australian Warning System (AWS) & CAP normalization across all jurisdictions.
  - Multi-platform sensor suite:
    - `geo_location` entities with dynamic lifecycle synchronization and native Home Assistant map plotting.
    - `binary_sensor` entities for active threats, emergency warnings, road closures, and power outages.
    - `sensor` entities for active hazard count, highest warning level, closest incident distance, nearest incident title, fire danger ratings, air quality, local weather observation, road closures count, power outages count, weather warnings count, and relief facilities.
    - `camera` platform providing live flood camera and traffic webcam snapshot streaming.
  - Custom Lovelace dashboard cards (`ausnz-disaster-card` / `aus-disaster-card`).
  - Professional branding package in `branding/` (`icon.png`, `logo.png`, `icon.svg`).
  - Strict 100% test coverage across all integration files, zero lint warnings/errors, and complete validation with Home Assistant 2026 standards.
